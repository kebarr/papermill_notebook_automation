## What actually drives cost

- **Every kernel starts at about 1–1.3 GB** before any analysis (the lowest per-call peaks in your runs). On the 3 GB server, that leaves well under 2 GB for work.
- **PCA:** the number of samples and the amount of data read dominate; the number of SNPs barely matters. In your `plot_pca_n_snps` sweep, the `3.0` release PCA (~2,500 samples, 26 Mbp of 3L) peaked at **4.5–4.9 GB for every value from 10k to 100k SNPs**. The same call capped at 20 samples peaked at ~2.1–2.4 GB, a 50-sample BF-A PCA at ~2.3 GB, and France (`AG1000G-FR`) at ~1.4–1.6 GB.
- **Selection scans (G123, H12, iHS, XP-EHH):** with 10–50-sample cohorts these took seconds and peaked at about 1.5–3.5 GB. G123 with **no** cohort cap, i.e. all of BF-A's gambiae samples, reached **5 GB**. Cost grows with samples × sites read.
- **Network and cache matter more than CPU on first runs.** The first PCA run took **868 s** versus ~150 s later, because it downloaded data that later runs read from the local cache. On a fresh server, the first run of anything is mostly download time.
- **XP-EHH uses at most 4 threads, and iHS at most 2.** That's a hard limit in `scikit-allel` (`ThreadPool(min(4, cpu_count))`). So 15 or 30 CPUs don't speed up a single scan; they let you run several at once.
- **PCA has a hard ceiling around 20,000 samples on every server,** from the 32-bit maths library in your scipy (the karyotype error), however much RAM you add.

**Rules of thumb that follow:**
- **Memory headroom:** keep the Mac-measured peak below roughly 60–70% of a server's RAM, to allow for Linux's higher counts.
- **Threads:** set `THREADS` (or `DASK_NUM_WORKERS`) to match the server: 1 on the two small servers, 2 on the 12 GB server, and around 8 on the large ones. Otherwise Dask starts one thread per core it detects, and memory grows with it.

**The research reasoning behind those ranges:**
- **Cohort size for selection statistics:** about 20 per population is the minimum for G123/H12/XP-EHH to be meaningful (the notebooks' `min_cohort_size`); 50–100 is typical in Ag1000G scans. So the 6 GB server handles real single-population analyses, while larger studies start at 12 GB.
- **PCA:** one sample set answers within-region questions; `3.0`-scale answers continent-scale population structure. 10k SNPs already resolves the major structure, and your sweep shows more SNPs cost almost nothing extra. Spend capacity on samples and region size instead.
- **`filter_min_maf` and `min_ehh`:** 0.05 is the usual choice and suits every server. Values of 0.01 (rarer variants, longer haplotypes) are worth it for older or weaker selection signals, and belong on the CPU-rich servers.
